# Media Coverage Report: Oct 8, 2026 PERM Suspension Announcement

## Summary
On October 8, 2026, at a White House Fraud Task Force press conference (South Court Auditorium / Eisenhower Executive Office Building) led by VP JD Vance with Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling, AG Todd Blanche, Stephen Miller, and DOL IG Anthony D'Esposito, the Trump administration announced it is suspending Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata (TCS), Wipro, HCL, and Capgemini from the DOL Permanent Labor Certification (PERM) program — the required first step for most employment-based green cards. The administration will "not accept any new or process any pending" PERM applications involving the companies (Sonderling), citing alleged fraud; Vance accused Microsoft of laying off ~6,000 American workers while obtaining 6,300 H-1B visas and ~3,000 green cards. The same event announced J-1 visa-fraud investigations (subpoenas served) at nine universities: Harvard, Yale, Stanford, Brown, U. Pittsburgh, UC Davis, Caltech, MIT, Arizona State.

Coverage is still in the first hours (research cutoff ~17:25 UTC / 1:25 pm ET Oct 8). Found coverage at: **CNN, NY Post, AP (wire), Reuters, Fox Business, Barron's, Newsmax, USA Today, Washington Examiner, CNBC (indirectly), ZeroHedge**, plus Indian outlets (Outlook Business, Gulte, Daily Jagran, Inshorts) and immigration press (VisaVerge, 7globe.in). **Not found: Axios, Newsweek, WSJ, Bloomberg, Politico, The Hill** — no coverage located via repeated targeted searches; likely not yet published. **No company has responded** (Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys all "did not immediately respond"). **No White House transcript and no published DOL action document/enforcement order** were located.

## Fact-checking observed
- **Newsmax** (only outlet doing so systematically): Vance's remarks "did not establish that each visa or green card represented a new hire replacing a laid-off worker"; no data cited for wage claims; no duration/pending-application specifics given; J-1 stats unexplained.
- **AP / Doug Rand:** called the policy "incoherent" on its own terms — green cards *free* H-1B workers from employer dependence, so blocking PERM deepens the "indentured servitude" problem Vance claims to oppose.
- **VisaVerge:** holds are not denials; Adobe's Sept 25 snapshot gave no reason or timetable.
- **Gulte:** the 230,000 H-1B approvals / 100,000 PERM certifications since 2009 span 17 years and do not equal current affected workers; actual pending-PERM headcount not disclosed.
- No outlet independently verified the 6,000/6,300/3,000 figures against USCIS/DOL data, nor the Rapid Response 47 "3,682 PERM / ~1,000 same-position" claim.
